What Happens When You Inherit Property in Bulgaria?

pixel_pilot
15.07.2026 • Read time: 7 min

Inheriting property in Bulgaria can be a complex legal and administrative process, especially for foreign nationals. Whether you are a local resident or an heir living abroad, understanding the Bulgarian Inheritance Act and the specific requirements for real estate transfer is crucial to securing your assets. This guide outlines the essential steps, tax obligations, and legal nuances you must navigate to successfully claim your inherited property.

The Legal Framework of Inheritance in Bulgaria

In Bulgaria, inheritance is governed by the Law on Inheritance. Upon the death of a property owner, the estate is transferred to the heirs according to either a valid will or, in the absence of one, the legal order of succession. The process is not automatic in terms of ownership records; you must actively prove your right to inherit and register the property in your name.

Key Steps to Claim Inherited Property

  • Obtain the Death Certificate: This is the primary document required to initiate the inheritance process.
  • Certificate of Heirs (Удостоверение за наследници): Issued by the local municipality, this document lists all legal successors. It is the most critical document for any future legal transactions.
  • Declaration of Inheritance: You must declare the inherited property to the local National Revenue Agency (NRA) office within six months of the death.
  • Registration at the Registry Agency: To officially update the title deed, you must record the inheritance at the Registry Agency (Property Register) in the district where the property is located.

Tax Obligations for Inheritors

Bulgaria has specific rules regarding inheritance tax. While direct descendants (children and spouses) are generally exempt from inheritance tax, collateral relatives (siblings, nephews, nieces) and non-relatives are subject to tax rates determined by the local municipality. It is vital to consult with a local accountant to ensure your financial transactions and potential future sales are fully compliant with Bulgarian tax laws.

Heir Category Tax Status
Spouse, Children, Grandchildren Exempt
Siblings and their children Subject to local tax rates
Other persons Subject to higher local tax rates

Managing and Maintaining Your Inherited Property

Once you have legally acquired the property, you must decide whether to keep it, rent it out, or sell it. If you choose to keep the property, you must consider ongoing maintenance costs, including municipal property taxes and utility management. Many heirs find that they need to upgrade the facility to make it habitable or marketable. For instance, understanding the best heating options for houses in Bulgaria is essential for ensuring comfort during the cold winter months and increasing the long-term value of your asset.

Commercial Potential of Inherited Assets

If you have inherited a property that is zoned for commercial use or is located in a high-traffic area, you might consider converting it into a business asset. Navigating the regulations for business use requires a deep understanding of local zoning laws. If you are considering this route, it is highly recommended to consult a comprehensive guide to buying and managing commercial property in Bulgaria for business to ensure you remain compliant with local commercial building codes and licensing requirements.

Common Challenges for International Heirs

Foreigners inheriting property in Bulgaria often face hurdles regarding language barriers and document legalization. If documents are issued outside of Bulgaria, they must be translated by a certified translator and, in many cases, apostilled to be recognized by Bulgarian authorities. Engaging a local lawyer who specializes in inheritance law is highly recommended to expedite the process and avoid common pitfalls in the Property Register.

Summary Checklist for Heirs

  • Secure the Death Certificate.
  • Request the Certificate of Heirs from the municipality.
  • File the inheritance declaration with the National Revenue Agency.
  • Settle any outstanding municipal property taxes.
  • Record the change of ownership at the Registry Agency.
  • Assess the property for potential renovations or commercial use.

Inheriting property in Bulgaria can be a rewarding endeavor if handled with due diligence. By following the legal procedures and ensuring all documentation is filed correctly, you can secure your ownership and maximize the value of your inherited estate.
