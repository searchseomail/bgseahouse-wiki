What are Act 14, Act 15, and Act 16 in Bulgarian Real Estate?

copper_fox
20.06.2026 • Read time: 6 min

Navigating the Bulgarian real estate market can be an intimidating process for foreign investors and first-time buyers. One of the most unique aspects of purchasing property in Bulgaria is the specific terminology used to describe the construction stages of a building. Understanding the distinction between Act 14, Act 15, and Act 16 is not just a formality; it is a critical step in ensuring your investment is secure and legally compliant. Whether you are considering a luxury apartment in a resort or looking into whether Sunny Beach real estate is a good investment, knowing the status of the building is paramount.

What is Act 14? The Structural Milestone

Act 14 is the first major legal milestone in the construction of a building in Bulgaria. It marks the completion of the "rough construction" phase, which includes the structural framework of the building. Once a building receives Act 14, it is officially recognized as a structure with walls and a roof.

  • What it includes: The foundation, columns, floor slabs, and external walls are completed.
  • Legal significance: At this stage, the building is officially entered into the property cadastre. Most importantly, this is the point at which an individual apartment can be sold via a Notarial Deed.
  • Risk factor: Buying at the Act 14 stage is often cheaper, but it carries a higher risk, as the internal finishing, plumbing, and electrical works are yet to be completed.

What is Act 15? The Completion of Construction

Act 15 represents the completion of the building’s construction. At this stage, the developer has finished all technical installations, including plumbing, electrical wiring, HVAC systems, and the exterior facade. The building is essentially ready for occupancy, but it is not yet officially certified for "habitation."

It is important to note that when calculating your budget, you must account for these stages, as the hidden costs of buying property in Bulgaria explained in our guide often involve utility connections and final administrative fees that occur around this time.

What is Act 16? The Certificate of Habitation

Act 16 is the final and most important document for any residential property in Bulgaria. It is officially known as the "Permission for Use" (Разрешение за ползване). This document is issued by the National Construction Control Directorate (DNKC) and certifies that the building is fully compliant with all safety, health, and fire regulations.

Why Act 16 matters:

  • Utility Rates: Without Act 16, a building is often forced to use "industrial" electricity and water rates, which are significantly higher than residential rates.
  • Legal Safety: You cannot legally register your permanent address at the property until the building has received its Act 16.
  • Property Value: Properties with Act 16 are considered "finished" and are generally more expensive and easier to finance through a mortgage.

Final Considerations for Buyers

Before finalizing your purchase, it is essential to conduct thorough due diligence. Always ask the developer or the seller for a copy of the specific Act they claim the building has achieved. Do not rely on verbal promises, as the transition from Act 15 to Act 16 can sometimes be delayed by bureaucratic hurdles.

Furthermore, keep in mind that once you take possession of your property, you will face ongoing expenses. Understanding the real cost of property maintenance in Bulgaria is vital for long-term owners, especially in complexes that require year-round security, pool maintenance, and landscaping. By understanding these three Acts, you are better equipped to make an informed decision and protect your assets in the Bulgarian market.
