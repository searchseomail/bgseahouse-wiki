Common Real Estate Scams in Bulgaria and How to Avoid Them

urban_wanderer
12.06.2026 • Read time: 6 min

Bulgaria’s real estate market has seen significant growth over the past decade, attracting international investors drawn by affordable coastal properties and mountain retreats. However, with this popularity comes an increase in fraudulent activities targeting unsuspecting foreign buyers. Understanding the risks is essential for anyone asking, is it safe to buy real estate in Bulgaria right now? The answer is yes, provided you approach the transaction with due diligence and professional legal guidance.

The Most Common Real Estate Scams in Bulgaria

Scammers often exploit a lack of local knowledge, language barriers, and unfamiliarity with Bulgarian property law. Being aware of the following tactics is your first line of defense.

1. The "Off-Plan" Development Trap

Many investors are lured by the prospect of buying property at a lower price before it is built. While many developers are legitimate, some take deposits for projects that are never completed, or worse, never intended to break ground. In some cases, the developer may use the funds from new buyers to cover debts from previous failed projects, creating a "Ponzi" scheme cycle. To avoid unvetted projects, buyers can inspect direct developer developments with verified track records at this link.

2. Property Title Fraud

This is perhaps the most dangerous scam. A fraudster may pose as the legitimate owner of a property by using forged documents or by exploiting a deceased owner’s property that has not yet been formally inherited by the rightful heirs. If you purchase such a property, you may find yourself in a lengthy legal battle to prove ownership, or you could lose the property entirely when the true heirs come forward.

3. Hidden Liens and Debts

In Bulgaria, debts can be attached to the property rather than the person. If you purchase a home without a thorough check of the "Act for Encumbrances," you might inherit the previous owner's unpaid utility bills, bank mortgages, or even active foreclosure proceedings initiated by creditors.

4. Inflated Prices and "Ghost" Agents

Unscrupulous agents may collude with sellers to artificially inflate the property price, pocketing the difference as an illegal commission. Always research the best places to buy real estate in Bulgaria to ensure you have a baseline for market value in your target area.

How to Protect Yourself: A Checklist for Buyers

Avoiding scams requires a methodical approach to due diligence. Never feel pressured to sign documents or transfer large sums of money quickly.

  • Hire an Independent Lawyer: Never use the lawyer recommended by the seller or the real estate agency. Hire your own legal counsel to perform a comprehensive background check on the property title.
  • Verify the Property Register: Ensure your lawyer checks the Property Register (Imoten Registar) to confirm there are no existing mortgages, injunctions, or legal disputes associated with the property.
  • Request a Certificate of Encumbrances: This document is critical. It provides a historical summary of the property and reveals any third-party claims.
  • Understand the Financial Implications: Beyond the purchase price, be aware of ongoing costs. It is vital to spend time understanding property taxes in Bulgaria to avoid future penalties from the local municipality.

Comparison of Red Flags vs. Safe Practices

Red Flags Safe Practices
Pressure to pay a deposit immediately. Taking time to review all documents with a lawyer.
The seller insists on using their own notary or lawyer. Appointing your own independent legal representation.
Prices significantly lower than the market average. Conducting a comparative market analysis.
Vague or missing documentation for the property. Verifying the Notary Deed and the Property Register.

Final Thoughts on Buying Safely

While the Bulgarian real estate market offers incredible potential, it is not a "wild west." By maintaining a healthy level of skepticism and ensuring that every stage of the transaction is verified by an independent expert, you can navigate the market with confidence. Always prioritize transparency, demand formal documentation, and never rush into a deal that feels "too good to be true."
