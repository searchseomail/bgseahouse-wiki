Bulgaria Real Estate

A Guide to Finding Repossessed Properties in Bulgaria

silent_synapse
16.07.2026 • Read time: 7 min

The Bulgarian real estate market has long attracted international investors and lifestyle seekers, particularly those looking for value. Among the various acquisition strategies, purchasing repossessed properties—often referred to as bank-seized assets or auction properties—stands out as a way to secure significant discounts. However, the process is markedly different from a standard residential purchase and requires a deep understanding of the local legal framework.

Understanding the Repossession Process in Bulgaria

In Bulgaria, the repossession process is primarily driven by private enforcement agents (known as Chasten sudeben izpalnitel or CSI). When a property owner defaults on a mortgage or other debts, the creditor initiates an enforcement case. The CSI then puts the property up for a public auction to recover the outstanding debt.

It is important to note that these auctions are highly regulated. The goal of the process is to ensure transparency, though the procedural complexity can be daunting for foreigners. Unlike standard sales, you are dealing with a court-appointed official rather than a private seller.

Where to Find Repossessed Property Listings

Finding these deals requires knowing exactly where to look. Because these properties are sold through legal enforcement, they are not always listed on standard real estate portals like Imot.bg or Alo.bg. Instead, you must monitor specific channels:

  • The Register of Public Auctions: The official Chamber of Private Enforcement Agents maintains a searchable database of all active auctions across the country.
  • Private Enforcement Agent Websites: Many top-tier law firms and enforcement agencies list their active portfolios directly on their corporate websites.
  • Local Newspapers: By law, auction notices must be published in local newspapers, making physical archives a surprisingly effective tool for tracking upcoming sales in specific regions.

Key Steps to Secure an Auction Property

If you are looking to invest in a budget-friendly home—perhaps as an alternative to top places to retire in Bulgaria for expats—you must follow a strict procedure:

Step Action
1. Due Diligence Check the property for existing tenants or pending legal claims.
2. Deposit Pay a 10% guarantee deposit to the enforcement agent.
3. Bidding Submit your bid in a sealed envelope before the deadline.
4. Closing If you win, pay the remaining balance within the statutory timeframe.

Legal Risks and Considerations

Buying a repossessed property is not without its pitfalls. One major issue is the "hidden" status of the property. You might find that the previous owner still occupies the unit, or that there are outstanding utility bills linked to the property that the new owner might be pressured to settle. Furthermore, you must ensure that your investment is protected, and once you have secured the title, you should immediately look into property insurance in Bulgaria: costs and coverage explained to safeguard your new asset against unforeseen damages.

Is the Property Vacant?

In Bulgaria, a successful bid at an auction does not automatically grant you physical possession. If the previous owner refuses to leave, you may be required to initiate a separate legal eviction process. This is a crucial factor to account for in your budget, as it can delay your ability to move in or rent out the property by several months.

The Role of Local Legal Counsel

It is highly recommended that you hire a Bulgarian attorney who specializes in enforcement law. They can help you navigate the nuances of the "Decree for Awarding Property," which is the document that serves as your title deed. While some buyers attempt to handle the paperwork alone, the risks of inheriting undisclosed liens or procedural errors during the auction process make professional legal representation a vital investment.

Conclusion

Finding repossessed properties in Bulgaria offers a unique pathway to property ownership at a fraction of the market rate. By utilizing the official registers, conducting thorough due diligence, and preparing for the legal complexities of the auction system, you can successfully add a valuable asset to your portfolio. Whether you are seeking a vacation home or a long-term investment, the Bulgarian auction market remains a goldmine for those who are well-prepared.
