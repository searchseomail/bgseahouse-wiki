How to Buy Property in Bulgaria as a Foreigner

shadow_scribe
31.05.2026 • Read time: 5 min

Bulgaria has emerged as a premier destination for international property investors and retirees seeking a high quality of life at an affordable price point. With its stunning Black Sea coastline, majestic mountain resorts, and rapidly modernizing cities like Sofia and Plovdiv, the country offers diverse opportunities. However, navigating the legal landscape of buying property in Bulgaria as a foreigner requires a clear understanding of local regulations, bureaucratic processes, and tax implications.

Understanding Legal Eligibility for Foreigners

The ability to purchase property in Bulgaria depends largely on your citizenship and the type of property you intend to acquire. Following Bulgaria's accession to the European Union, the rules were simplified, but distinctions remain.

EU and EEA Citizens

Citizens of the European Union, the European Economic Area, and Switzerland enjoy the same rights as Bulgarian citizens when purchasing property. They can buy both residential properties (apartments and houses) and land (including plots of land associated with houses) without any specific restrictions.

Non-EU Citizens

Non-EU citizens face slightly different regulations regarding land ownership:

  • Buildings and Apartments: Non-EU citizens can freely purchase apartments and houses without land (or with the ideal part of the land under the building).
  • Land Ownership: Under the Bulgarian Constitution, non-EU individuals cannot own land directly. To circumvent this, many foreign investors register a Bulgarian limited liability company (OOD). The company acts as a legal entity and can purchase and own land, including agricultural or forest land, in its own name.

The Step-by-Step Purchasing Process

Buying property in Bulgaria follows a structured legal path designed to protect both the buyer and the seller. It is highly recommended to engage a local independent lawyer to conduct due diligence.

1. Due Diligence and Property Search

Before signing anything, ensure your lawyer performs a "Property Check" at the Property Registry. This confirms that the seller is the legal owner and that the property is free of mortgages, liens, or ongoing litigation.

2. The Preliminary Contract

Once the legal check is clear, a preliminary contract is signed. This document outlines the terms of the sale, the price, and the completion date. At this stage, the buyer typically pays a deposit, usually 10% of the purchase price, to take the property off the market.

3. Notary Deed and Completion

The final transfer of ownership must be executed in front of a Bulgarian Notary Public in the district where the property is located. The notary verifies all documentation, ensures all local taxes are paid, and officially records the transfer in the Property Registry.

Associated Costs and Taxes

Budgeting correctly is essential. Beyond the purchase price, you must account for additional fees that typically range between 4% and 6% of the property value.

Expense Type Estimated Cost
Notary Fees 0.1% - 1.5% (sliding scale)
Local Tax 2.0% - 3.0% (varies by municipality)
Registry Agency Fee 0.1%
Legal Fees €500 - €1,500 (depending on complexity)

Taxation and Ongoing Maintenance

Once you own property in Bulgaria, you are subject to specific ongoing obligations:

  • Annual Property Tax: Usually between 0.01% and 0.45% of the tax valuation of the property.
  • Garbage Collection Fee: Calculated based on the tax value of the property and set annually by the local municipality.
  • Rental Income Tax: If you decide to rent out your property, Bulgaria has a flat income tax rate of 10%.

Essential Tips for Foreign Buyers

Hire an Independent Lawyer

Never rely solely on the lawyer recommended by the real estate agent or the seller. An independent legal representative ensures that your interests are prioritized and that the paperwork is beyond reproach.

Power of Attorney (PoA)

If you cannot be present for the entire process, you can grant a Power of Attorney to your lawyer or a trusted representative. This document must be notarized and, in some cases, apostilled if signed outside of Bulgaria.

Opening a Bank Account

It is advisable to open a Bulgarian bank account to handle the transaction and pay your future utility bills. While the purchase can be made via international transfer, a local account simplifies the management of the property significantly.

Conclusion

Buying property in Bulgaria is an accessible process provided you follow the correct legal channels. By understanding the distinction between purchasing as an individual versus a company, accounting for all associated fees, and ensuring thorough due diligence, you can secure a valuable asset in one of Europe’s most promising real estate markets. Always prioritize professional legal advice to ensure your investment remains secure for years to come.
