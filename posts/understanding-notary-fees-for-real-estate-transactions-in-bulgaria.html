Understanding Notary Fees for Real Estate Transactions in Bulgaria

shadow_scribe
26.07.2026 • Read time: 6 min

Navigating the Bulgarian real estate market can be an exciting venture, whether you are a local investor or a foreigner looking for a holiday home. However, the legal process involves several mandatory costs, with notary fees being among the most significant. If you are currently debating your housing strategy, it is worth comparing the long-term financial implications of renting vs buying an apartment in Sofia to ensure your investment aligns with your financial goals.

How Notary Fees are Calculated in Bulgaria

In Bulgaria, notary fees are not arbitrary. They are strictly regulated by the "Tariff for Notary Fees" under the Notaries and Notarial Practice Act. The cost of the notary deed is calculated based on the material interest, which typically refers to the higher value between the property's tax assessment (issued by the municipality) and the actual purchase price stated in the preliminary contract.

The calculation follows a progressive scale. As the value of the property increases, the percentage charged by the notary decreases. It is important to note that these fees are subject to 20% VAT, which must be added to the final calculation.

The Notary Fee Scale (Simplified)

Property Value (BGN) Base Fee Percentage on Excess
Up to 1,000 30 BGN 1.5%
1,001 – 10,000 45 BGN 1.3%
10,001 – 50,000 162 BGN 0.8%
50,001 – 100,000 482 BGN 0.5%
100,001 – 500,000 732 BGN 0.2%
Over 500,000 1,532 BGN 0.1% (up to 6,000 BGN max)

Additional Costs Beyond Notary Fees

While the notary fee covers the authentication of the deed, the total closing costs are higher. Buyers should be prepared for the following mandatory expenses:

  • Local Tax: Paid to the municipality where the property is located. This typically ranges between 0.1% and 3% of the property value, depending on the specific municipality’s regulations.
  • Registration Fee: Paid to the Registry Agency (Property Register) to record the transaction. This is usually 0.1% of the property value.
  • Legal Fees: If you hire an independent lawyer to conduct due diligence or review the preliminary contract, you will need to factor in their hourly or flat-rate fees.

Hidden Costs of Property Ownership

Once the notary fees are paid and the property is legally yours, it is important to remember that the financial commitment does not end at the closing table. Many first-time buyers underestimate the real cost of property maintenance in Bulgaria, which includes building management fees, insurance, and potential renovation funds. Furthermore, you should proactively research understanding utilities cost in Bulgaria for an apartment to ensure you can comfortably manage your monthly outgoings once you move in.

Pro-Tips for Managing Transaction Costs

  • Request a Tax Assessment: Always check the official tax assessment (данъчна оценка) early, as this is the baseline for your notary fee calculations.
  • Clarify Fee Responsibility: By local custom, the buyer usually covers the notary and registration fees, while the seller covers the costs of providing the necessary documents. However, these terms can be negotiated in the preliminary contract.
  • Payment Methods: Be aware that for transactions exceeding 10,000 BGN, Bulgarian law requires payments to be made via bank transfer to ensure transparency and compliance with anti-money laundering regulations.

Understanding these costs upfront prevents last-minute financial stress. By working with a reputable real estate agent and a qualified lawyer, you can ensure that your property transaction in Bulgaria is handled professionally and that all mandatory notary fees are calculated accurately.
