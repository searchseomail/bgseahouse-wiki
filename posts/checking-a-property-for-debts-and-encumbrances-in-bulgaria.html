Checking a Property for Debts and Encumbrances in Bulgaria

orbit_seeker
31.07.2026 • Read time: 6 min

Purchasing real estate in Bulgaria is an exciting prospect, whether you are seeking a vacation home by the Black Sea or a strategic investment in Sofia. However, the Bulgarian property market carries specific legal risks that every buyer must navigate carefully. Among the most critical steps in the due diligence process is verifying whether a property is burdened by debts, mortgages, or legal encumbrances. Failing to conduct a thorough check could result in inheriting the previous owner's financial liabilities or, in the worst-case scenario, losing your investment entirely.

Understanding Property Encumbrances in Bulgaria

An encumbrance is a claim or liability attached to a property that may lessen its value or obstruct its transfer. In the context of Bulgarian real estate, these are typically recorded in the Property Register (Imoten Registar). Common types of encumbrances include:

  • Mortgages: Loans secured by the property itself.
  • Distraints (Seizures): Legal orders placed by creditors or the National Revenue Agency (NRA) due to unpaid debts.
  • Right of Use (Usufruct): A legal right granted to a third party to inhabit or use the property.
  • Pending Litigation: Claims regarding the ownership status of the property currently being heard in court.

If you are exploring the market, it is essential to be aware of the risks associated with distressed assets. For instance, if you are looking for a bargain, you should read our comprehensive guide to finding repossessed properties in Bulgaria, which details how these legal statuses affect the buying process.

The Role of the Registry Agency

The primary tool for verifying a property's status is the Certificate of Encumbrances (Udoystoverenie za tezhesti). This official document is issued by the Registry Agency and provides a historical record of all registered claims against a specific real estate asset. It is important to note that this certificate only covers registered encumbrances; some debts, such as unpaid municipal taxes or utility bills, may not appear on this document.

To obtain this certificate, your lawyer or notary public will submit a request to the Registry Agency. The document typically shows:

Encumbrance Type Description
Contractual Mortgage A voluntary agreement between the owner and a bank.
Legal Mortgage Imposed by law, often during specific debt recovery processes.
Distraint A freeze on the property due to court-ordered debt collection.

What Happens If You Find Debts?

Discovering a mortgage or a distraint does not necessarily mean the deal is off. Many properties in Bulgaria are sold while under mortgage. In such cases, the process involves a tripartite agreement between the buyer, the seller, and the seller’s bank. The funds from the sale are first used to pay off the mortgage, the bank issues a document clearing the debt, and the property is then transferred to the new owner free of encumbrances.

If you are planning to finance your purchase through a local bank, it is helpful to understand the specific requirements for non-residents. You can learn more about this in our article on whether foreigners can get a mortgage in Bulgaria.

Essential Due Diligence Checklist

Beyond the official Certificate of Encumbrances, buyers should perform a holistic check to ensure they are not falling victim to fraudulent activities. The Bulgarian property market, while regulated, still requires vigilance. Consider these steps:

  • Verify the Seller’s Identity: Ensure the person selling the property is the legal owner or has a valid Power of Attorney.
  • Check for Unpaid Taxes: Request a document from the local municipality proving that all property taxes for the current and previous years have been paid.
  • Utility Bills: Ask for proof that electricity, water, and heating bills are up to date.
  • Consult a Lawyer: Never rely solely on the notary public. An independent lawyer acts in your best interest to uncover hidden risks.

By staying diligent, you can protect yourself from common real estate scams in Bulgaria and how to avoid them. Taking the time to perform a thorough legal audit of the property will save you from significant financial and emotional distress down the road.

Conclusion

Checking a property for debts and encumbrances is the most vital defensive move you can make in the Bulgarian real estate market. While the legal process can seem daunting for international buyers, working with a reputable real estate attorney and ensuring that all official documents are thoroughly inspected will provide the peace of mind necessary to complete your purchase successfully. Always prioritize the Certificate of Encumbrances and ensure that all financial obligations are cleared prior to the final signing of the Title Deed.
