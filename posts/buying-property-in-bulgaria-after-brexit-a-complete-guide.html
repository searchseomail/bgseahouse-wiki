Buying Property in Bulgaria After Brexit: A Complete Guide

velvet_echo
25.07.2026 • Read time: 6 min

For many British citizens, the dream of owning a holiday home or a retirement sanctuary in the sun remains as strong as ever. However, since the United Kingdom’s withdrawal from the European Union, the process of buying property in Bulgaria has shifted from a seamless intra-EU transaction to a slightly more regulated procedure. While the path has changed, Bulgaria remains one of the most affordable and attractive real estate markets in Europe. This guide outlines everything you need to know to navigate the post-Brexit landscape successfully.

Can British Citizens Still Buy Property in Bulgaria?

The short answer is yes. Brexit has not stripped British citizens of the right to own real estate in Bulgaria. However, the legal mechanism for ownership depends on the type of property you intend to purchase:

  • Apartments: British citizens can purchase apartments in their own name without restriction. Since an apartment does not include ownership of the underlying land, the process remains straightforward.
  • Houses with Land: This is where the post-Brexit rules apply. Non-EU citizens are generally restricted from owning land in Bulgaria. To purchase a house with a garden, you must register a Bulgarian company (an EOOD or OOD). The company acts as a legal Bulgarian entity, which then holds the title to the property and the land.

The Legal Process: A Step-by-Step Overview

Navigating the legalities requires diligence. Many buyers find it helpful to consult a comprehensive guide on the purchasing process to ensure they comply with local regulations. Generally, the steps include:

  1. Property Search and Reservation: Once you find your ideal home, you sign a preliminary contract and pay a reservation fee.
  2. Due Diligence: Your lawyer will conduct a title search to ensure there are no encumbrances, mortgages, or legal disputes attached to the property.
  3. The Notary Deed: The transfer of ownership is finalized in the presence of a Bulgarian notary, where the final deed is signed and registered.

Crucial Considerations for Post-Brexit Buyers

Understanding Property Status

When searching for your dream home, you will frequently encounter terms like Act 14, Act 15, and Act 16. It is vital to understand that what Act 14, Act 15, and Act 16 mean in Bulgarian real estate determines the legal habitability of a building. Never purchase a property that has not received its Act 16 (permission to use) if you intend to live there permanently.

Budgeting Beyond the Purchase Price

Many first-time buyers focus solely on the sticker price of the property, forgetting that acquisition comes with additional expenses. Before committing, ensure you have researched the hidden costs of buying property in Bulgaria, including notary fees, municipal taxes, and agency commissions.

Security and Market Stability

Prospective investors often ask about the current climate, especially regarding the geopolitical situation in Eastern Europe. If you are wondering if it is safe to buy real estate in Bulgaria right now, it is helpful to look at the stability of the local market and the long-term trends of foreign investment in the region.

Final Advice for UK Buyers

While Brexit has added a layer of administrative work for those interested in houses with land, the Bulgarian property market remains exceptionally welcoming to British investors. To protect your investment:

  • Always hire an independent lawyer who is not affiliated with the seller or the estate agent.
  • Ensure all funds are transferred through official banking channels to create a clear audit trail.
  • Consider the long-term maintenance costs and the potential for holiday rental income if the property will be used as a second home.

By conducting thorough research and working with local experts, British citizens can continue to enjoy the high quality of life and stunning landscapes that Bulgaria offers.
