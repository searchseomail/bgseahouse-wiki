How to Register a Company in Bulgaria to Buy Land

flora_fable
22.07.2026

Bulgaria has emerged as a premier destination for international investors seeking affordable real estate within the European Union. However, one of the most common questions for non-EU citizens—and even some EU nationals—is whether they can purchase land directly. While apartments can be bought by individuals, agricultural land and certain types of plots often require a Bulgarian legal entity. Understanding how to buy property in Bulgaria involves navigating the local legal landscape, and for many, registering a company is the most efficient path forward.

Why Register a Bulgarian Company for Land Acquisition?

According to the Bulgarian Constitution and the Law on Ownership, foreign individuals from outside the EU/EEA are generally restricted from owning land (specifically agricultural land and forests) in their personal capacity. Registering a Bulgarian Limited Liability Company (OOD or EOOD) effectively grants you the status of a local legal entity, bypassing these restrictions. Once incorporated, the company is treated as a Bulgarian citizen under the law, allowing it to purchase any type of real estate, including plots, villas, and commercial developments.

Types of Companies for Foreign Investors

When starting your business journey in Bulgaria, you will primarily choose between two structures:

  • EOOD (Sole Owner Limited Liability Company): Owned by a single person. This is the most popular choice for individual investors.
  • OOD (Limited Liability Company): Owned by two or more partners.

Both structures offer limited liability, meaning your personal assets remain protected from company debts.

The Step-by-Step Registration Process

Registering a company in Bulgaria is a streamlined process that can often be completed within 3 to 5 business days, provided all documentation is prepared correctly.

1. Choosing a Name and Registered Office

You must check the Bulgarian Trade Register to ensure your desired company name is available. Additionally, you need a registered office address. This address will be the official point of contact for tax authorities and legal correspondence.

2. Preparing the Incorporation Documents

You will need to draft the Articles of Association or the Incorporation Act. These documents outline the company’s scope of business, management structure, and share capital distribution.

3. Capital Deposit

The minimum share capital for a Bulgarian OOD/EOOD is symbolically low—just 2 BGN (approximately 1 EUR). You must open a "cumulation" bank account in a local Bulgarian bank to deposit this capital before the company is officially registered.

4. Registration in the Trade Register

Once the documents are notarized and the capital is deposited, the application is submitted to the Bulgarian Trade Register (Registry Agency). Upon approval, your company receives a unique identification number (EIK), which is essential for all future property transactions.

Financial Considerations and Ongoing Costs

It is important to understand that owning a company comes with ongoing administrative responsibilities. Even if the company is used solely as a vehicle for holding land, you must maintain annual accounting.

Expense Item Estimated Cost
Company Registration Fees 150 - 300 EUR
Notary & Legal Fees 200 - 500 EUR
Annual Accounting 500 - 1,000 EUR/year

Key Considerations After Acquiring Land

Once your company successfully purchases the property, your focus should shift to maintenance and protection. Whether you are looking at the best sea resorts for buying property in Bulgaria or rural agricultural plots, safeguarding your investment is paramount. Ensure you research property insurance in Bulgaria: costs and coverage explained to mitigate risks associated with natural disasters or structural damage.

Conclusion

Registering a Bulgarian company is a strategic move for investors aiming to diversify their portfolio with land ownership. While the administrative requirements might seem daunting at first, the benefits—such as full ownership rights, tax efficiency, and EU market access—make it a worthwhile endeavor. Always consult with a local legal expert to ensure your company formation is compliant with the latest Bulgarian legislation.
