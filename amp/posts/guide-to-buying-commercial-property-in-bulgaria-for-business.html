Guide to Buying Commercial Property in Bulgaria for Business

ember_drift
05.07.2026

Bulgaria has emerged as a premier destination for international investors looking to expand their operations within the European Union. With a favorable tax regime, a strategic geographical location, and competitive operational costs, the country offers a robust environment for commercial ventures. However, navigating the local real estate market requires a deep understanding of legal frameworks, regional nuances, and financial obligations.

Why Invest in Commercial Property in Bulgaria?

The Bulgarian commercial real estate market spans diverse sectors, including office spaces in major hubs like Sofia and Plovdiv, retail units in bustling urban centers, and industrial warehouses near logistics corridors. Investors are often drawn to the country's 10% corporate tax rate, which remains among the most attractive in the EU.

Before committing to a purchase, it is vital to perform thorough due diligence. While many investors are tempted by low entry prices, it is crucial to research whether cheap property in Bulgaria is really worth it in the long run, considering potential renovation requirements or structural issues that could impact your business operations.

Key Steps in the Commercial Acquisition Process

Purchasing commercial real estate involves more complexity than residential buying. You must ensure the property's zoning classification matches your intended business use. Whether you are looking for office space, a boutique hotel, or a manufacturing facility, the legal status of the building is paramount.

  • Market Research: Identify high-growth areas based on your business model.
  • Legal Due Diligence: Verify the property title, absence of mortgages, and existing lease agreements.
  • Tax and Financial Planning: Consult with a local accountant to understand the VAT implications and local taxes associated with commercial assets.

For a comprehensive roadmap on the legal requirements and documentation necessary for foreign buyers, you can refer to this ultimate step-by-step guide on how to buy property in Bulgaria.

Financial Considerations and Hidden Expenses

When budgeting for a commercial acquisition, the purchase price is only the beginning. Many first-time investors fail to account for secondary costs that can significantly impact the final return on investment. It is essential to be fully aware of the hidden costs of buying property in Bulgaria, including notary fees, local taxes, brokerage commissions, and necessary legal representation fees.

Typical Costs Breakdown

Expense Type Estimated Range
Notary Fees 0.1% – 1.5% of the property value
Local Municipality Tax 2% – 3% depending on the region
Agency Commission 2.5% – 3% + VAT
Legal Representation 1% – 2% depending on complexity

Legal Structure for Business Ownership

In Bulgaria, foreigners can buy commercial property as individuals or through a local entity. Most businesses opt to register a Bulgarian Limited Liability Company (OOD or EOOD). This structure simplifies the acquisition process, allows for the deduction of operational expenses, and facilitates the recovery of VAT on commercial real estate investments.

Important Considerations for Foreign Investors:

  • Commercial Zoning: Ensure the property has the correct "Purpose of Use" (e.g., office, shop, warehouse). Changing this status post-purchase can be a lengthy bureaucratic process.
  • Lease Agreements: If the property is being sold with sitting tenants, review the existing contracts thoroughly to understand your rights and obligations as the new landlord.
  • Energy Efficiency: Modern Bulgarian legislation requires buildings to meet specific energy standards, which can impact your utility costs and future resale value.

Conclusion

Investing in commercial property in Bulgaria is a strategic move that can yield significant long-term benefits if approached with caution and professional guidance. By conducting thorough due diligence, understanding the tax landscape, and preparing for all associated costs, you can successfully establish a stable and profitable commercial base in Southeastern Europe.
