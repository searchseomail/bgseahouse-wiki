How to Safely Transfer Money to Buy Property in Bulgaria

26.06.2026

Purchasing real estate in Bulgaria is an attractive prospect for international investors, thanks to the country's low tax rates, affordable property prices, and beautiful landscapes. However, navigating the financial aspects of an international property transaction requires careful planning and security measures. Whether you are looking at a vacation home or considering how to get residency in Bulgaria by buying property, understanding the legal and banking framework for transferring funds is the first step toward a successful acquisition.

Understanding the Legal Framework for Property Payments

Bulgaria operates under strict anti-money laundering (AML) regulations, which are aligned with European Union directives. When buying property, you must be prepared to prove the origin of your funds. Most transactions involving international buyers are conducted via bank transfers rather than cash, as Bulgarian law imposes strict limits on cash payments for high-value transactions.

Key Documentation Requirements

  • Proof of Origin: Banks may require documents such as salary slips, tax returns, or sale contracts of assets in your home country.
  • Source of Wealth Declaration: You will likely be asked to fill out a "Know Your Customer" (KYC) form at your chosen Bulgarian bank.
  • Property Sale Contract: The preliminary or final contract acts as the legal basis for the transfer of funds.

Step-by-Step Guide to Transferring Funds

To ensure your money arrives safely and reaches the seller without unnecessary delays, follow these professional steps:

1. Open a Local Bank Account

While it is technically possible to transfer funds directly from your home bank to the seller’s account, opening a Bulgarian bank account is highly recommended. It provides a transparent record of the transaction and simplifies future payments like property taxes, utility bills, and maintenance fees.

2. Use Professional Currency Exchange Services

Bulgaria uses the Lev (BGN), which is pegged to the Euro. If you are transferring funds from a non-Euro currency (like GBP or USD), using a high-street bank often results in poor exchange rates and high fees. Specialized currency brokers often provide better rates and faster transfer speeds, which is particularly beneficial when buying off-plan property in Bulgaria: a complete guide to risks and benefits, where multiple payment stages are involved.

3. Verify the Seller’s Details

Before initiating any transfer, confirm the recipient's bank details with your lawyer. Fraudulent activity involving "fake" bank accounts is a risk in international real estate. Always ensure the bank account belongs to the legal owner of the property as stated in the Notary Act.

Managing Risks and Avoiding Common Pitfalls

Safely transferring money is not just about the technical process; it is about protecting your investment. Never transfer the full purchase price until your lawyer has performed a thorough title search and confirmed there are no encumbrances, such as mortgages or liens, against the property. If you ever decide to exit the market, knowing the legalities of how to sell a property in Bulgaria from abroad will be essential to ensure you can repatriate your funds safely.

Essential Tips for Security:

  • Use an Escrow Account: If you are uncomfortable transferring money directly to the seller, ask your legal representative if they can facilitate an escrow service.
  • Keep All Records: Retain every SWIFT confirmation, invoice, and bank statement. These will be necessary for your tax filings in your home country.
  • Coordinate with a Lawyer: A local property lawyer in Bulgaria acts as your safeguard. Do not skip this step to save on fees; the cost of legal protection is negligible compared to the risk of a failed transfer.

By following these protocols, you can navigate the Bulgarian property market with confidence, ensuring that your capital is transferred safely, legally, and cost-effectively.
