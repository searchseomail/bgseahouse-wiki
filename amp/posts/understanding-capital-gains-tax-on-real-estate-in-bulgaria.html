What is Capital Gains Tax in Bulgaria?

In the context of real estate, capital gains tax is a levy imposed on the profit realized from the sale of an asset. In Bulgaria, the tax regime is relatively straightforward, but it is governed by the Personal Income Tax Act (PITA). For most individuals, the profit is defined as the difference between the selling price and the acquisition price (the price at which you bought the property), minus any documented expenses related to the acquisition and sale.

Exemptions: When You Don't Have to Pay

One of the most attractive features of the Bulgarian tax system is the generous exemption policy for primary residences. You may be exempt from paying capital gains tax under the following conditions:

  • Primary Residence: You do not owe tax on the profit from the sale of one residential property per year, provided that the property has been owned for more than 3 years.
  • Multiple Properties: If you sell up to two residential properties in a single tax year, the profit from the sale of the second property is taxable, but the first one remains exempt (subject to the 3-year ownership rule).
  • Inherited Properties: Real estate acquired through inheritance or restitution is generally exempt from capital gains tax regardless of how long you have owned it.

Tax Rates for Non-Exempt Sales

If your property sale does not qualify for an exemption, the profit is considered taxable income. For individuals, the flat tax rate in Bulgaria is 10%. This is significantly lower than in many other European Union countries, making it a competitive destination for those tracking current real estate market trends in Bulgaria.

How to Calculate Your Taxable Gain

To calculate the tax, use the following logic:

Taxable Base (Selling Price) - (Acquisition Price + Deductible Expenses).

Reporting and Deadlines

Even if you believe your sale is exempt, it is essential to comply with Bulgarian tax filing requirements. Residents and non-residents alike must declare the sale in their Annual Tax Return (submitted between January 10th and April 30th of the following year). Failure to report the transaction can lead to penalties, regardless of whether tax is actually owed.
