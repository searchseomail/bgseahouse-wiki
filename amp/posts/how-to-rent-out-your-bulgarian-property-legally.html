How to Rent Out Your Bulgarian Property Legally

orbit_seeker
20.07.2026

Bulgaria has become an increasingly popular destination for real estate investment, thanks to its affordable prices, stunning landscapes, and growing tourism sector. However, transitioning from a property owner to a landlord requires strict adherence to local regulations. Whether you are renting out a holiday home on the Black Sea coast or an apartment in Sofia, understanding the legal framework is essential to avoid fines and ensure a smooth income stream.

Understanding the Legal Status of Your Property

Before you list your property on platforms like Airbnb or Booking.com, you must ensure your property is registered for commercial use. In Bulgaria, this is often referred to as categorizing your property for tourist accommodation.

  • Categorization: You must apply for a categorization certificate from the local municipality or the Ministry of Tourism. This applies if you intend to offer "tourist accommodation services."
  • The Unified Tourist Information System (ESTI): All landlords providing short-term accommodation are legally required to register in the ESTI system. This allows the government to track tourist stays in real-time.
  • Safety Standards: Your property must meet specific health, safety, and fire protection requirements to receive its official star rating.

Tax Obligations for Landlords in Bulgaria

Rental income in Bulgaria is subject to personal income tax. As a property owner, you are responsible for declaring this income in your annual tax return. Generally, the tax rate for rental income is 10%, after deducting a normative expenditure rate of 10% (resulting in an effective tax rate of 9% on gross income). It is highly recommended to speak with a local accountant to ensure your Bulgarian bank account is set up correctly to handle these transactions and tax payments efficiently.

Protecting Your Investment

Being a landlord involves risks, from property damage to liability claims if a guest gets injured. Before welcoming your first tenant, ensure you have adequate financial protection in place. Comprehensive property insurance in Bulgaria is not just a safety net; it is a critical requirement for maintaining a professional rental business and mitigating unexpected repair costs.

Key Operational Considerations

Requirement Description
Rental Agreement Always have a written contract detailing the duration, price, and house rules.
Property Maintenance Regular upkeep is vital. If you are renovating an old house to make it rental-ready, ensure all electrical and plumbing work meets current standards.
Tourist Tax Collect the mandatory tourist tax from guests and remit it to the municipality.

Managing Tenants and Compliance

When renting out your property, you act as the primary point of contact. If you live outside of Bulgaria, you may consider hiring a professional property management company. They can handle the check-in process, ESTI reporting, and maintenance requests on your behalf. However, remember that as the owner, you remain legally responsible for ensuring that all guests are registered with the police within 24 hours of arrival if they are foreign nationals.

Final Checklist for New Landlords

  • Obtain your categorization certificate from the local mayor's office.
  • Register your property in the ESTI system.
  • Draft a robust rental contract reviewed by a Bulgarian lawyer.
  • Set up a dedicated accounting process for declaring rental income.
  • Ensure your property insurance is active and covers short-term rental activities.

By following these steps, you can turn your Bulgarian property into a reliable source of passive income while staying fully compliant with the law. While the administrative burden might seem significant at first, the long-term benefits of a legal and well-managed rental property far outweigh the initial effort.
