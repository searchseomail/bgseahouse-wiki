How to Sell a Property in Bulgaria From Abroad

midnight_tide
21.06.2026

Selling a property in Bulgaria while living abroad may seem like a daunting logistical challenge, but with the right preparation and legal framework, it is a straightforward process. Whether you are divesting from a holiday home or offloading a long-term investment, understanding the Bulgarian real estate landscape is essential for a smooth transaction.

Key Preparations Before Listing Your Property

Before you list your home on the market, you must ensure your documentation is in order. Bulgarian property law is strict regarding ownership verification. You will need your original Title Deed (Notary Act), a current certificate of tax assessment, and an up-to-date certificate for the lack of encumbrances (issued by the Registry Agency).

If you have previously considered how to get residency in Bulgaria by buying property, ensure that your residency status or citizenship details do not conflict with tax obligations upon the sale. You should also verify that all utility bills are paid in full, as outstanding debts are tied to the property and can stall the closing process.

Working with Professionals from a Distance

Since you are abroad, appointing a local representative is a strategic necessity. You have two primary options:

  • Power of Attorney (PoA): You can grant a trusted individual or a lawyer the power to sign documents on your behalf. This document must be notarized and, depending on your country of residence, may require an Apostille.
  • Professional Real Estate Agency: Partnering with a reputable agency can handle viewings, negotiations, and the coordination of the final notary signing.

Be vigilant during this stage. Unfortunately, the market has its risks, so it is wise to research common real estate scams in Bulgaria and how to avoid them to ensure your assets and identity remain protected throughout the sale.

The Financial and Tax Implications

Selling a property in Bulgaria involves specific tax considerations, particularly for non-residents. Capital gains tax is generally applicable unless the property has been your primary residence for a certain period or meets specific exemption criteria under the Bulgarian Personal Income Tax Act.

Marketing Your Property to Global Buyers

To attract the right buyer, your listing must stand out. High-quality photography and virtual tours are essential when most of your potential buyers are also looking from abroad. If your property is located in a popular tourist destination, emphasize the lifestyle benefits. For instance, if you are selling in a mountain resort, it helps to understand the current market trends, such as is buying property in Bansko for winter rentals a good investment, as this can help you frame the selling points for potential buy-to-let investors.

Closing the Deal

The final step is the signing of the Notary Deed. With a valid Power of Attorney, your representative can attend the notary appointment in Bulgaria to finalize the transfer. Once the transaction is signed, the funds are usually transferred to your bank account via a secure escrow or direct bank transfer. Ensure that your Bulgarian bank account remains open until the funds are successfully cleared to avoid currency exchange complications.

Checklist for Remote Sellers:

  • Gather all original property deeds and land registry documents.
  • Obtain a notarized Power of Attorney for your chosen representative.
  • Settle all outstanding property taxes and utility arrears.
  • Consult with a tax accountant regarding your tax liability in both Bulgaria and your country of residence.
  • Prepare high-resolution media for online listings to reach international buyers.
