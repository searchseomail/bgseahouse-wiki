Can I Buy Agricultural Land in Bulgaria as an Expat?

orbit_seeker
27.06.2026

For many expatriates, the allure of Bulgaria lies in its fertile landscapes, affordable rural properties, and the dream of sustainable living. However, the legal framework surrounding land ownership in this Balkan nation can be complex. If you are wondering whether you can buy agricultural land in Bulgaria as an expat, the answer depends heavily on your citizenship and your business structure.

The Legal Framework: EU vs. Non-EU Citizens

Bulgaria’s accession to the European Union in 2007 brought significant changes to property laws. The rules governing the acquisition of agricultural land are distinct from those governing residential apartments or houses. Understanding these nuances is a critical step in how to buy property in Bulgaria as a foreigner.

  • EU Citizens and EEA Residents: Individuals and legal entities from EU member states (or EEA countries) generally enjoy the same rights as Bulgarian citizens regarding land acquisition, provided they have resided in Bulgaria for a certain period or meet specific agricultural registration requirements.
  • Non-EU Citizens: As a general rule, citizens of countries outside the EU/EEA cannot purchase land (including agricultural plots) in their personal name. This restriction is rooted in the Bulgarian Constitution, which limits land ownership to Bulgarian citizens and EU/EEA entities.

How Non-EU Expats Can Bypass Ownership Restrictions

If you are a non-EU citizen but are determined to acquire agricultural land, the most common and legally sound method is to establish a Bulgarian limited liability company (known as an OOD or EOOD). By registering a company in Bulgaria, you create a legal entity that is considered a "Bulgarian person" in the eyes of the law. This entity can then purchase agricultural land, forests, and other real estate without the restrictions placed on individual foreign nationals.

Key Requirements for Company Ownership

While forming a company is an effective workaround, it comes with responsibilities:

  • Accounting and Taxes: Your company must maintain formal accounting records and file annual tax returns, even if it is not actively trading.
  • Maintenance Costs: You will need to account for costs related to company registration, annual audits, and administrative fees.
  • Land Use Regulations: Agricultural land is protected under strict zoning laws. You must ensure that the land remains classified as agricultural unless you go through the complex process of changing its status.

Factors to Consider Before Buying

Before you commit to a purchase, you should weigh the agricultural potential against the logistical realities. Many expats look toward the best places to buy real estate in Bulgaria, but agricultural land often comes with different considerations than residential seaside or city property.

Soil Quality Bulgaria has high-quality chernozem soil, but fertility varies significantly by region.

The Role of the Notary

Regardless of whether you are buying as an individual or through a company, the transaction must be finalized before a Bulgarian notary. The notary's role is to verify the title deeds, ensure the land is free of encumbrances (such as mortgages or legal disputes), and confirm that all taxes are paid. For those looking for the top places to retire in Bulgaria for expats, investing in land can be a rewarding project, but it is highly recommended to hire a local lawyer to perform thorough due diligence.

Conclusion: Is It Worth the Effort?

Buying agricultural land in Bulgaria is a feasible goal for expats, provided you navigate the legal requirements correctly. While non-EU citizens face hurdles, the company formation route is a well-trodden path that allows for full ownership rights. Before proceeding, always consult with a professional who specializes in Bulgarian property law to ensure your investment is protected and that your plans align with local land-use regulations.
